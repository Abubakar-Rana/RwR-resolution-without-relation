Reasoning for Ultra-High-Resolution
Remote-Sensing Vision-Language Models
Hafiz Muhammad Abubakar

Abstract—Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote-sensing im-
agery provides detailed information about small objects and
their spatial configuration, but its extreme image dimensions
pose challenges for vision-language models (VLMs). Direct image
resizing can remove fine-grained visual evidence, while indepen-
dent tile-based processing can preserve local detail but disrupt
spatial relationships between objects distributed across the scene.
Existing UHR approaches primarily address evidence acquisition
through resolution scaling, token selection, cropping, or active
visual exploration. In contrast, this work investigates whether
explicitly representing spatial relationships across independently
processed image tiles can improve relational reasoning.
We propose a lightweight cross-tile spatial-relation framework
that combines question-conditioned region selection, local VLM
reasoning, global coordinate transformation, and a geometric
scene graph representing relationships between objects across
tiles. Qwen2.5-VL-7B is used as the underlying VLM to provide a
controlled baseline. The proposed method is evaluated on XLRS-
Bench, with particular emphasis on object spatial relationship,
relational counting, and multi-region grounding tasks. Controlled
ablations compare direct inference, independent tiling, question-
conditioned selection, and the proposed cross-tile relation repre-
sentation under matched computational conditions.

I. INTRODUCTION
Recent advances in vision-language models (VLMs) have
enabled increasingly sophisticated visual understanding and
reasoning across natural images and domain-specific applica-
tions. Remote sensing presents a particularly challenging set-
ting because satellite imagery frequently contains large spatial
extents, dense object distributions, and fine-grained structures
that are difficult to preserve when images are processed at
conventional resolutions.
Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote-sensing imagery makes
this challenge more pronounced. XLRS-Bench introduced a
dedicated benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language
models on UHR remote-sensing scenes, with an average image
size of approximately8500×8500pixels and 16 subtasks
covering both perception and reasoning capabilities [1]. The
benchmark demonstrates that current multimodal models still
face substantial difficulties when required to interpret ex-
tremely large remote-sensing scenes.
A fundamental difficulty arises from the trade-off between
local detail and global context. Processing an entire UHR im-
age directly requires aggressive visual compression, which can
remove information about small objects. Conversely, dividing
the image into smaller tiles allows a model to inspect high-
resolution regions but can disconnect objects that occur in
different tiles. Consequently, a model may correctly identify
individual objects while failing to determine their relationships
at the scene level.
Recent research has primarily addressed the first part of
this problem. GeoLLaV A-8K introduced background token
pruning and anchored token selection to enable multimodal
models to process remote-sensing imagery at resolutions up
to8K×8K[2]. GeoEyes further introduced on-demand
visual focusing, allowing a model to actively zoom into
task-relevant regions [3]. GeoVista extended active perception
through global planning, branch-wise regional inspection, and
explicit evidence tracking across multiple regions [4]. These
approaches improve the acquisition and organization of visual
evidence in UHR scenes.
However, evidence acquisition does not necessarily provide
an explicit representation of geometric relationships between
objects identified in independently processed regions. This
distinction is important for tasks such as determining which
object is north of another object, which facilities fall within
a particular region, or how objects distributed across distant
image tiles are spatially related.
Relational reasoning has previously been explored in
remote-sensing visual question answering. EarthVQA intro-
duced a relational reasoning framework that explicitly mod-
els object semantics and external spatial relationships us-
ing segmentation-derived representations and object-guided
attention [5]. However, EarthVQA addresses conventional-
resolution remote-sensing imagery rather than the cross-tile
reasoning problem introduced by extremely large UHR scenes.
This work therefore investigates the following research
question:
Does explicit cross-tile spatial-relation representa-
tion improve VLM performance on relational rea-
soning tasks in ultra-high-resolution remote-sensing
imagery?
We hypothesize that representing objects in a common
global coordinate system and explicitly constructing their spa-

tial relationships will improve relational tasks while producing
smaller gains on tasks that primarily require single-object
perception or generic captioning.
The main contributions of this study are:
1) We formulate cross-tile spatial-relation reasoning as a
specific challenge for UHR remote-sensing VLMs.
2) We introduce a lightweight geometric representation that
connects objects extracted from independently processed
tiles through a common global coordinate system.
3) We conduct controlled ablation and compute-matched
experiments to isolate the contribution of explicit cross-
tile spatial relationships.
4) We provide task-level and qualitative analysis to deter-
mine whether improvements are concentrated on rela-
tional reasoning tasks.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Ultra-High-Resolution Remote-Sensing VLMs
The development of UHR remote-sensing benchmarks has
highlighted limitations of existing multimodal models when
processing very large satellite images. XLRS-Bench intro-
duced manually annotated UHR imagery with an average
image size of approximately8500×8500pixels and defined
16 subtasks covering perception and reasoning capabilities
[1]. The benchmark provides a direct evaluation setting for
studying the effect of image resolution and scene complexity
on remote-sensing VLMs.
GeoLLaV A-8K addressed the computational challenges as-
sociated with UHR imagery through Background Token Prun-
ing and Anchored Token Selection. The method exploits
the observation that important visual information is often
concentrated in object-centric regions and enables processing
at resolutions up to8K×8K[2]. These approaches primarily
address the problem of preserving or efficiently accessing
useful visual information under extreme image dimensions.
B. Active Visual Exploration for UHR Remote Sensing
A second line of research treats UHR interpretation as an
active perception problem. GeoEyes introduced on-demand
visual focusing using a staged training strategy and reinforce-
ment learning to improve adaptive zooming and evidence
acquisition [3]. GeoVista subsequently introduced planning-
driven active perception, branch-wise regional inspection, and
explicit evidence tracking to reduce incomplete exploration
and duplicated evidence [4].
These methods demonstrate the importance of selecting and
inspecting relevant regions rather than treating the complete
UHR image uniformly. Nevertheless, their primary objective
is evidence acquisition and tracking. The present work focuses
on a complementary question: after evidence has been acquired
from multiple regions, how should the spatial relationships
among objects distributed across those regions be represented
for final reasoning?

C. Relational Reasoning in Remote Sensing
Relational reasoning has also been investigated in remote-
sensing VQA. EarthVQA introduced a multi-task dataset
containing 6,000 images and 208,593 question-answer pairs
and proposed a Semantic Object Awareness framework
for object-centric relational reasoning [5]. The framework
uses segmentation-derived object representations and cross-
attention to model object relationships.
While EarthVQA establishes the importance of explicit
object relationships for remote-sensing reasoning, it does not
specifically address the spatial discontinuity created when an
extremely large image is divided into independently processed
tiles. Our work investigates this intersection between UHR
visual processing and explicit geometric relational reasoning.
III. DATASET
A. XLRS-Bench
XLRS-Bench is used as the primary evaluation benchmark
[1]. The benchmark contains extremely large remote-sensing
images with an average spatial dimension of approximately
8500×8500pixels and evaluates multimodal models across
16 subtasks.
The benchmark covers both perceptual and reasoning ca-
pabilities. For this study, particular attention is given to tasks
that require relationships between multiple objects or spatial
regions, including Object Spatial Relationship, relational and
regional counting, and multi-region grounding.
Non-relational tasks such as object perception and caption-
ing are also retained for comparison. This allows the study to
determine whether the proposed method produces task-specific
improvements rather than uniformly improving all types of
visual understanding.

The proposed framework consists of four principal stages:
global scene understanding, question-conditioned tile selec-

reasoning.

VI. EXPERIMENTALDESIGN
Four configurations are evaluated.
A. Direct VLM
The original image representation is provided directly to
Qwen2.5-VL-7B without tile-level processing.
B. Independent Tiling
The image is divided into high-resolution tiles and pro-
cessed independently. No explicit cross-tile spatial representa-
tion is provided.
C. Question-Conditioned Tiling
Question-conditioned region selection is introduced before
local VLM reasoning, but no explicit spatial relation graph is
constructed.

VII. ABLATIONSTUDIES
The primary ablation removes the cross-tile relation repre-
sentation while keeping the VLM, tile selection mechanism,
image regions, and computational budget unchanged.
Additional experiments investigate the effects of:
•tile size,
•tile overlap,
•number of selected tiles,
•object localization accuracy,
•relation types,
•computational budget.
The central hypothesis predicts that removing the relation
representation will primarily affect tasks requiring relation-
ships among spatially separated objects.

XII. DISCUSSION
The central objective of this study is not to develop a new
foundation VLM, but to isolate the contribution of explicit
spatial relation representation in UHR remote-sensing reason-
ing.
If the proposed method produces its largest improvements
on relational tasks while providing smaller improvements on
non-relational tasks, this would support the hypothesis that
cross-tile relation loss represents a distinct limitation of tile-
based UHR reasoning.
Conversely, if improvements are observed uniformly across
all tasks, the results would suggest that the observed benefit
may arise from more general improvements in visual evidence
acquisition rather than from the proposed relational mecha-
nism.

XIII. CONCLUSION
This study proposes a cross-tile spatial-relation reasoning
framework for ultra-high-resolution remote-sensing vision-
language understanding. The framework combines question-
conditioned region selection, local high-resolution visual rea-
soning, global coordinate transformation, and geometric rela-
tion construction.
The research is designed to determine whether explic-
itly preserving spatial relationships between objects across
independently processed image tiles can improve relational
reasoning in UHR remote-sensing scenes.
